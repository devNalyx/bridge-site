Bridge_

Coordinate humans and AI agents as one software team.

Bridge is the operational orchestration layer for hybrid software teams — it routes work, carries context, enforces policy, and knows exactly when to bring a human in. Not another coding agent. Not another dashboard. The layer that decides what happens next.

[PRE-LAUNCH]

The problem

Multiple agents can already execute work — implement, review, test, investigate. What's missing is the layer that coordinates them: deciding who acts next, carrying context between participants, enforcing policy on what can happen automatically, and knowing when a human needs to step in. Today that layer is you: copy-pasting results between terminals, re-explaining context to the next agent, deciding by hand who should act next.

The solution

Bridge is not a webhook relay with a product wrapped around it. It's the control plane: given what just happened in your repo, Bridge decides who or what should act next, with what context, under what policy — and whether it needs you first.

How it works

The first proven workflow: implementer → Bridge → reviewer → Bridge → human.

  1. An implementation agent builds a change and opens a pull request.
  2. Bridge detects the event and routes it to the reviewer role, with the context it needs attached.
  3. The reviewer acts — no copy-pasting between terminals.
  4. Changes requested → Bridge hands it back to the implementer, automatically.
  5. Review passes → Bridge checks policy: does this need your approval?
  6. You're notified only when your judgment is actually the next step.
$ bridge watch --repo owner/repo

[implementer] opened PR #142 "Add rate limiting to /api/ingest"
  → routed to reviewer · context attached (diff, linked issue, prior notes)

[reviewer] requested changes — rate limit window is hardcoded
  → routed back to implementer

[implementer] pushed a fix, re-requested review
[reviewer] approved
  → policy: security-sensitive path → human approval required
  # notified you · waiting on your call

Core capabilities

Roles

Implementer, reviewer, security reviewer, tester, human approver — every participant has a defined responsibility and bounded authority.

Events

PR opened, review requested, CI failed, approval needed — Bridge reacts to what actually happened in your repo, not a poll loop.

Context

A handoff carries what the next participant needs to act, not just a notification that something happened.

Handoffs

Work moves from implementer to reviewer to human automatically, with nothing lost in translation.

Policies

Routine work proceeds automatically. Security-sensitive changes and ambiguous calls stop for a human — by policy, not by accident.

Human escalation

You're brought in exactly when your judgment has the highest value, not for every step.

Auditability

Handoffs, approvals, and actions are traceable — a system you can trust with real repos.

Bridge runs entirely outbound — no open inbound ports, no tunnel to babysit — but that's plumbing in service of the above, not the point of the product.

Why Bridge

Because the future of AI-assisted development isn't one perfect agent doing everything — it's several specialized agents and humans, and someone has to make them work together well. That's the harder problem, and it's the one Bridge is built around. Bridge doesn't compete with your coding agents, your GitHub, or your issue tracker — it complements all of them. What it replaces is manual coordination between autonomous participants.

Vision

Today you think: "which terminal do I need to open next, and which agent do I need to tell what?" Bridge's goal is for you to think instead: "the system knows who's responsible for the next step."

Agents execute. Bridge coordinates. Humans decide where judgment matters.

Longer term, the same operational position that lets Bridge coordinate a workflow lets it learn from one: which reviewers care about what, which agents perform well on which tasks, where human intervention is actually needed. That's a future capability Bridge earns by proving the coordination loop first — not a claim about what exists today.
