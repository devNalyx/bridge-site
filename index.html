The problem

Your agents can already implement, review, and test. What they can't do is coordinate themselves — so you do it by hand: telling one agent what another decided, re-explaining context across terminals, deciding who acts next.

Bridge is not a webhook relay with a product wrapped around it. It's the control plane: given what just happened in your repo, Bridge decides who or what should act next, with what context, under what policy — and whether it needs you first.

Why it's different

Stop being the glue

Bridge watches your repo's events and routes them to the right participant — human or agent — automatically.

Context travels with the work

A handoff carries what the next participant needs to act, not just a notification that something happened.

You stay in command

Policy defines what's automatic and what waits for you. Bridge escalates judgment calls; it doesn't make them for you.

Every decision is traceable

Handoffs, approvals, and actions are auditable — a system you can trust with real repos.

What Bridge coordinates

Roles, not just tools

Implementer, reviewer, security reviewer, tester, investigator, human approver — every participant has a defined responsibility and bounded authority.

Event-driven coordination

PR opened, review requested, CI failed, approval needed — Bridge reacts to what actually happened in your repo, not a poll loop.

Policy-aware automation

Routine work proceeds automatically. Security-sensitive changes and ambiguous calls stop for a human — by policy, not by accident.

Zero-port delivery

Your machine only ever dials out. Events reach your local agents over an encrypted, outbound-only stream — no open ports, no manually managed tunnels.

How it works

The first proven workflow: implementer → reviewer → human.

  1. An implementation agent builds a change and opens a pull request.
  2. Bridge detects the event and routes it to the reviewer role.
  3. The reviewer acts with the context it needs — no copy-pasting between terminals.
  4. Changes requested → handed back to the implementer, automatically.
  5. Review passes → Bridge decides whether this needs your approval.
  6. You're notified only when your judgment is actually the next step.

Why Bridge

Because the future of AI-assisted development isn't one perfect agent doing everything — it's several specialized agents and humans, and someone has to make them work together well. That's the harder problem, and it's the one Bridge is built around.

Vision

Today you think: "which terminal do I need to open next, and which agent do I need to tell what?" Bridge's goal is for you to think instead: "the system knows who's responsible for the next step."

Agents execute. Bridge coordinates. Humans decide where judgment matters.
